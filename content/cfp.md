Robot swarms, IoT and edge–cloud systems, decentralised applications, socio-technical platforms, and networks of LLM-based agents are all made of many interacting computational entities, and what matters most is what they do collectively. Engineering them means facing the micro–macro link: how do we program the individual (micro) parts so that the intended collective (macro) behaviour emerges reliably, and how do we reason about behaviour that spans several scales of space, time, and organisation?

Several programming-language communities have each developed their own answers: macroprogramming and aggregate computing, choreographic and multiparty languages with endpoint projection, multi-tier programming, agent-oriented, organisational, and holonic programming, and, more recently, neuro-symbolic and differentiable programming. These approaches share key ideas, such as global views, projection, roles, collective state, and nesting, but their communities seldom meet. SLEMuX aims to bring them together at ECOOP 2027.
